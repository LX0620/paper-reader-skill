# Empirical and econometric papers

Reconstruct the unit of observation, population, sampling, time window, treatment/exposure, outcomes, controls, estimand, and estimation sample. Locate the exact variable definitions and regression specification. Check whether exposure and outcome timing supports the stated interpretation.

Explain the identification argument before discussing significance. Distinguish descriptive association, prediction, causal inference, and a proposed mechanism. Ask what assumptions identify the claimed effect and what evidence supports those assumptions in this study. Judge fixed effects, instrumental variables, difference-in-differences, selection correction, or other designs within their stated application; do not treat the method's name as proof of identification.

Read table headers, sample sizes, reference categories, units, transformations, standard-error/cluster choices, confidence intervals, and notes from the original visual table. For nonlinear models, distinguish coefficients from marginal effects/probability differences. Distinguish adjusted and unadjusted results, and distinguish within-group significance from a direct test of a between-group contrast. Do not treat an interaction coefficient in a nonlinear model as the entire interaction effect without the relevant calculation.

Assess robustness, measurement alternatives, timing, missingness, selection, heterogeneity, and multiple testing where relevant to the actual claim. A contemporaneous mediation regression alone does not establish a causal mechanism. A nonsignificant estimate may reflect uncertainty; examine interval width and substantive effect size. Do not conclude two interventions are synergistic merely because their joint-use group has a larger point estimate.

Translate findings into claims that match the evidence. Any new hypothesis is an analyst proposal, not an established result from the paper. For each proposed extra test, explain which specific uncertainty it resolves; avoid a generic inventory of every econometric technique.

# Theory and optimization papers

Identify decision makers, decision sequence, information available at each decision time, decision variables, exogenous parameters, objective, constraints, and uncertainty. Separate assumptions from derived properties. Explain why the proposed formulation is needed instead of only naming its techniques.

For each central result, record its exact statement and location, assumptions, quantifiers, domain, and boundary cases. Check whether the paper establishes existence, uniqueness, local or global optimality, or only a sufficient condition. Distinguish a fixed-parameter result from a uniform result. For minimax/interchange or differentiability arguments, inspect the stated conditions rather than assuming them.

Read the proof and relevant appendix before criticizing a missing step. Verify equations against page images when available. Reconstruct the important derivation as an explanatory argument; identify any step not independently checked. Symbolic/numerical checks are optional and require available tools and appropriate scope. Label computational checks as such. Never infer correctness from the fact that a solver returned an answer.

Explain the economic/managerial meaning of the property, the benchmark it improves on, and the assumptions under which that meaning holds. For inventory/operations papers, distinguish better demand prediction from better feasible decisions and measured cost/profit/service performance. Do not introduce censoring corrections, new constraints, or new mechanisms absent from the paper simply because they are possible extensions.
